<p><a href="http://lh6.google.com/sean.k.tallon/R3fy5VfpbUI/AAAAAAAAAgM/jzO0IXlO7c4/Slide2.JPG?imgmax=640"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer; width: 400px;" src="http://lh6.google.com/sean.k.tallon/R3fy5VfpbUI/AAAAAAAAAgM/jzO0IXlO7c4/Slide2.JPG?imgmax=640" alt="" border="0" /></a><br />Geosquan sent us a email last week for help from the readers of digital urban on his immersive 'hotdog' problem.<br /><br />Geo describes an immersive hotdog as:<br /><blockquote>a georeferenced linear collection of immersive photographs which create a seamless immersive environment which can be entered in a Geospatial Exploration System such as <a href="http://earth.google.com/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Earth</a>, <a href="http://www.microsoft.com/virtualearth/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Microsoft Virtual Earth</a>, <a href="http://www.esri.com/software/arcgis/explorer/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener">ESRI ArcGIS Explorer</a>, or <a href="http://worldwind.arc.nasa.gov/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">NASA’s WorldWind</a>. The shape of these immersive collections roughly resembles a hotdog.</blockquote>Moving from a series of panoramic images to a seamlessly stitched scene is the ultimate aim, we recommend <a href="http://geosquan.wordpress.com/2007/12/30/can-you-solve-the-immersive-hotdog-problem/">reading Geosquans full post</a> on his blog and then having a think about it...<br /><br />Its an interesting concept.</p>
