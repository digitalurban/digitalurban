<p><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RvoqVYE2l1I/AAAAAAAAArI/QE3EZ5ivbSA/s1600-h/mapmixer.jpg"><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5114446873561044818" style="DISPLAY: block; MARGIN: 0px auto 10px; CURSOR: pointer; TEXT-ALIGN: center" alt="" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RvoqVYE2l1I/AAAAAAAAArI/QE3EZ5ivbSA/s400/mapmixer.jpg" border="0" /></a><br />Looking back only two years ago if we wanted to upload a map via the web there would be all sorts of questions of copyright, overlay data, servers and a good few hours in a high end <span>GIS</span> system. Thankfully those days are long gone and uploading maps is getting easier and easier.<br /><br />Yahoo's Map Mixer is the latest development and is innovative allowing the placement of map overlays as simple as pointing Yahoo to a image and then adding in two control points. There is no need for a world co-ordinate file or even a working knowledge of <span>GIS</span> and <span>that's</span> how mapping should be (in our humble opinion).<br /><br />Once your image is overlaid it can then be embedded in a website or blog - as per below we illustrate our really quickly placed map of the <span>UCL</span> campus:<br /><br /><iframe src="http://maps.yahoo.com/mapmixer?h=300&w=490&lid=876a7f63&pg=showlayer" width="490" scrolling="no" height="300"></iframe><br /><br />While our map is not the most <span>geographically</span> accurate in the world it was created in 2 minutes.<br /><br />It is interesting to note Yahoo's views on maps:<br /><blockquote><span style="FONT-STYLE: italic"><span style="FONT-WEIGHT: bold">A word to the wise. These maps are meant to be fun, informational illustrations of places around the world. They may not be accurate, true to scale, factual, or official.</span></span></blockquote>We look forward to using the quote at a future mapping conference :)<br /><br /><a href="http://maps.yahoo.com/mapmixer">Take a look at Yahoo's Map Mixer</a>.</p>
