<img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5101540605274770914" style="DISPLAY: block; MARGIN: 0px auto 10px; CURSOR: hand; TEXT-ALIGN: center" alt="" src="/assets/uploads/external/3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RsxQJsCiTeI/AAAAAAAAAmI/TC4ivJIN0Yk/s400/NewYorktexturepanosm2.jpg" border="0" /><br />It is notably easier to create panoramas in Virtual Earth compared to our original <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2006/09/how-to-create-aerial-panorama-from.html">tutorial for Google Earth </a>due to the inclusion of the 'first person' navigation mode. First person mode allows the viewpoint to be rotated exactly from a single nodal point in a similar way to capturing panoramic images in the real world.<br /><br /><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5101513637175119298" style="FLOAT: right; MARGIN: 0px 0px 10px 10px; CURSOR: hand" alt="" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/Rsw3n8CiTcI/AAAAAAAAAl4/jUp8HWPWOW0/s400/NewyorkMorphed90.jpg" border="0" />This, combined with Microsoft's roll out of <span>photorealistic</span> cities, allows for the creation of stunning high resolution images in just a few simple steps:<br /><br /><strong>Step 1:<br /></strong><br />Load Virtual Earth by going to <a href="http://maps.live.com/">maps.live.com </a>and selecting 3D - type in your desired location and fly approximately 100 metres above the ground. For our example we choose New York due to the inclusion of 3D buildings;<br /><br /><strong>Step 2:</strong><br /><br />Place Virtual Earth into 'First Person' mode - Press the <span>CTRL</span>+Q keys on your keyboard. Moving the mouse will now allow you to rotate around a single point. Scroll the mouse wheel forward to decrease your altitude, and scroll the mouse wheel backward to increase your altitude.<br /><br /><strong>Step 3:<br /></strong><br />Grab screen shots of the view to ensure you have full coverage of the area. We used <a href="http://www.fraps.com/"><span>FRAPS</span></a> to grab the screen images but any method of screen capture will work.<br /><br />Depending on your screen resolution and aspect ratio it will take between 24 and 36 images - each image should allow 25% overlap to the previous capture. On a Dell 21 inch monitor at 1680x1050 pixels a complete panorama requires two rows of 12 images and a single final image looking down to complete the coverage.<br /><br /><strong>Step 4:<br /></strong><br />The images can be stitched in any stitching software, our tutorial uses <a href="http://www.ptgui.com/"><span>PTGui</span> </a>which is available as a 30 day trial download.<br /><br />Open <span>PTGui</span> and click 'Select Source Images', navigate to where you saved your <span>screengrabs</span> and select them all.<br /><br /><strong>Step 5:</strong><br /><br />Once the images are loaded a Camera Lens Dialogue will appear, simply click 'Cancel', your images will now be loaded ready to stitch.<br /><br /><strong>Step 6:</strong><br /><br />Click 'Align Images' <span>PTGui</span> will now analyse each image and automatically extract overlapping points, creating your final panorama. Once stitched simply click 'Create Panorama' and save at the optimum size.<br /><br />Your stitched panorama should resemble the image below:<br /><br /><div><p><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5101539943849807314" style="DISPLAY: block; MARGIN: 0px auto 10px; CURSOR: hand; TEXT-ALIGN: center" alt="" src="/assets/uploads/external/1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RsxPjMCiTdI/AAAAAAAAAmA/6LysdtopNyo/s400/NewYorktexturepanosm1.jpg" border="0" /><br /><strong>Step 7:</strong><br /><br />As Virtual Earth does not include clouds we can now add them in as a new layer in <span>photoshop</span>. We used a panoramic image of clouds from <a href="http://www.turbosquid.com/FullPreview/Index.cfm/ID/211719/SID/211692/blFP/1"><span>TurboSquid</span></a>, these used to be free but are now $5 each.<br /><br />If you don't have a cloud panorama then you can always create your own using <span>Terragen</span>, there is a<a href="http://www.3dna.net/cgi-bin/wwwthreads/showflat.pl?Cat=&Board=customization&amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;amp;Number=1398&page=0&amp;view=collapsed&sb=5&amp;part=1"> great tutorial on how to do this here</a>.<br /><br />Adding the clouds gives you the final panorama over New York:<br /><br /><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5101540605274770914" style="DISPLAY: block; MARGIN: 0px auto 10px; CURSOR: hand; TEXT-ALIGN: center" alt="" src="/assets/uploads/external/3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RsxQJsCiTeI/AAAAAAAAAmI/TC4ivJIN0Yk/s400/NewYorktexturepanosm2.jpg" border="0" /><br />By changing the projection of the image you can create a '<span>fisheye</span>' view of the city: </p><br /><p><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5101541331124243954" style="DISPLAY: block; MARGIN: 0px auto 10px; CURSOR: hand; TEXT-ALIGN: center" alt="" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RsxQz8CiTfI/AAAAAAAAAmQ/b0JHzzOrq_c/s400/NewYorktexturepanosm3.jpg" border="0" />A full resolution version of the <a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/andy/images/NewYorkpano.jpg">image above can be viewed from here</a> - <span>recommend</span>(6.9Mb .<span>jpg</span>).</p><br /><p>The panorama can also be converted for viewing at <span>Quicktime</span> Virtual Reality using <a href="http://gardengnomesoftware.com/"><span>Pano</span>2<span>QTVR</span></a>- which is an interesting although slightly pointless exercise. </p><br /><p><a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/andy/qt/NewYorkVE.html">View the panorama in <span>Quicktime</span> Virtual Reality </a>(4.4Mb)</p>Let us know if you create your own panoramic view....</div>
