<p>Twitter is a social network based on a simple web/phone <span>sms</span>/messenger text box asking simply 'what are you doing?' It is unique in terms of social networks as there are applications that allow 'Tweets' - that's Twitter for 'Posts' to be visualised geographically. As such it is no longer a social network existing in the ether such as <span>MySpace</span>, instead it is a unique <span>realtime</span>, global insight into what people are doing right now.<br /><br />In previous posts we have looked at <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2007/03/twitter-maps-unique-view-of-london.html">Twitter Maps</a> and <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2007/03/urban-twitters.html">Twitter Vision</a>, both interesting applications, allowing the geographic data posted with Twitter to be visualised. Yesterday we got a comment on the blog from Fresh Logic Studios, the developers of Atlas, about their new Twitter Visualisation:<br /><br /><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RhIjA_HQkgI/AAAAAAAAAOk/1h864WdyBFs/s1600-h/atlas1.jpg"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RhIjA_HQkgI/AAAAAAAAAOk/1h864WdyBFs/s320/atlas1.jpg" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5049136632084599298" border="0" /></a>Fresh <span>Logics</span> approach is to use <span>GeoRSS</span> and Atlas to load Twitter feeds every 5 seconds as an overlay to the mapping interface. This takes a step forward, compared to say <span>TwitterVision</span>, as it also provides a sidebar listing the latest Tweets.<br /><br /><a href="http://1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RhIkTPHQkhI/AAAAAAAAAOs/OwEHLa9Wt1c/s1600-h/atlas2.jpg"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RhIkTPHQkhI/AAAAAAAAAOs/OwEHLa9Wt1c/s320/atlas2.jpg" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5049138045128839698" border="0" /></a>To <span>emphasise</span> the geographic nature of the Twitter Network, if you select the 'Street View' option the interface zooms each Tweet to the local location. The above image is from a user in Vienna, complete with Tweet and location. The ability to view someones Tweet, image and location seconds after they have sent it is both fascinating and slightly worrying. Expect a lot of papers from Social Scientists in the near future. Come to think of it we have just included Twitter in a book chapter entitled The Visual City, coming soon...<br /><br />We will have more on Atlas in future posts, of note is their similar application using <span>Flickr</span>.<br /><br />Take a look at the <a href="http://atlas.freshlogicstudios.com/?GeoRssUri=http://atlas.freshlogicstudios.com/GeoRss/Feeds/Twitter.ashx&GeoRssUpdateInterval=5">Twitter Visualisation using Atlas</a> (warning you could spend hours just watching it).<br /><br />More about <a href="http://www.freshlogicstudios.com/">Fresh Logic Studios</a>.<br /><br /><span style="text-decoration: underline;"></span><a href="http://twitter.com/digitalurban">Join our Twitter network</a> for Tweets from DigitalUrban and fellow readers..</p>
