<p>Yesterday we looked at <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2007/05/simcity-ds-advertmovie.html">SimCity for the Nintendo DS</a> and the ability to create a sprawling metropolis on the hand held console. Taking the concept further and moving it into suburbia is life simulation computer game The Sims. Created by game designer <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Wright" title="Will Wright">Will Wright</a>, The Sims is the best selling game in PC history (<a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sims">Wiki - The Sims</a>), and as is common nowadays comes with its own building tools to create your own in game architecture.<br /><br />YouTube user <a href="http://www.youtube.com/profile?user=chrispycrunchy86">chrispycrunchy86</a> has posted a series of videos detailing how to build a house in The Sims. The movie below illustrates the construction and architecture of House Type 1:<br /><br /><object width="425" height="350"><param name="movie" value="http://www.youtube.com/v/CNRuK1v88Fo"></param><param name="wmode" value="transparent"></param><embed src="http://www.youtube.com/v/CNRuK1v88Fo" type="application/x-shockwave-flash" wmode="transparent" width="425" height="350"></embed></object><br /><br />As with many game sandboxes the tools are easy to use and within a short time you are able to built a unique structure. In 2002 <a href="http://www.maxis.com/">Maxis</a> shipped The Sims Online, with an online chat system, custom avatars and the ability to build it could in some ways be compared to <a href="http://www.secondlife.com">Second Life</a>. Interestingly the game was not a success. In today's world of social networks and Wikinomics (see our <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2007/04/book-review-wikinomics.html">book review</a>) The Sims online may of been before its time...</p>
