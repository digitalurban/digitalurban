Sometimes you walk to the end of lab, here in <a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/">CASA</a>, get introduced to a visitor and have to take a step back at the resulting demo. Yesterday i had the pleasure of meeting Tobias Franke of the <a href="http://www.esl.fim.uni-passau.de/page/">University of Passau's Embedded System Lab</a> who is part of a team looking at realtime crowd densities. Tobias has run a trial during the 2011 Lord Mayor's Show in London. The heatmap and the corresponding visualization was realised by Martin Wirz of the <a href="http://www.ife.ee.ethz.ch/people/wirzma">ETH Zurich's Wearable Computing Lab</a><span></span><span></span><a href="http://draft.blogger.com/"></a> with Prof. Eve Mitleton-Kelly (director of the <a href="http://www.psych.lse.ac.uk/complexity/aboutus/network.html">Complexity Group at the London School of Economics</a>) involved in the development as policy maker.  The overall system allows emergency services to gain an almost-live insight into the crowd density at large scale events with d<span style="background-color: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.917969); color: #222222;">ata has been collected and processed using the CoenoSense backand system (</span><a href="http://www.coenosense.com/" style="background-color: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.917969); color: #1155cc;" target="_blank" rel="noopener">www.coenosense.com</a><span style="background-color: rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.917969); color: #222222;">). T</span>he software has been developed as a part of the Socionical research project (for more information see <a href="http://www.socionical.eu/">socionical.eu</a>), in short we were impressed at the real time crowd viz via a smart phone app...</span>
