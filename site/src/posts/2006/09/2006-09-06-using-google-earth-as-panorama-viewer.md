<p><a href="http://photos1.blogger.com/blogger2/604/1219/1600/googlepano1.jpg"><img style="FLOAT: left; MARGIN: 0pt 10px 10px 0pt; CURSOR: pointer" alt="" src="/assets/uploads/external/photos1.blogger.com/blogger2/604/1219/320/googlepano1.jpg" border="0" /></a><br />*Edit - Kmz link now fixed - *<br /><br />While capturing screengrabs for our tutorial on <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2006/09/how-to-create-aerial-panorama-from.html">creating panoramas from Google Earth</a> we got thinking that you could use the Earth itself as a panoramic image viewer.<br /><br />All of our panoramas on the blog cover a 360x180 degree field of view - ie a sphere. By adding a new Google Earth overlay you can load a panoramic image and wrap it around the globe, thus allowing you to use the Earth as a viewer.<br /><br />As its Google Earth you can then add placemarks to navigate to buildings of interest, in our case the Swiss Re and Lloyds buildings in the City of London.<br /><br />Of course this is not all that practical, but it works... To try it out yourself <a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/andy/kmz/PanoViewer.kmz">download our kmz file</a> and take a look at the panorama of the City of London, or edit the image path and load up your own panoramic view...<br /><br />Thoughts or comments are as ever welcome.</p>
