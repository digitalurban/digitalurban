<p><a href="http://photos1.blogger.com/blogger2/604/1219/1600/agi_2006_logo_small.jpg"><img style="FLOAT: left; MARGIN: 0px 10px 10px 0px; CURSOR: hand" alt="" src="/assets/uploads/external/photos1.blogger.com/blogger2/604/1219/400/agi_2006_logo_small.jpg" border="0" /></a> A slow down on posts this week as we have been at the <a href="http://www.agi.org.uk/">Association of Geographical Information</a> 2006 conference presenting a paper on our use of <span>Goolge</span> Earth in Local Government. Of note, although sadly we missed the session on Google Earth due to other work, is that Google Earth is planning to include a 'Time Line' feature which should allow some level of animation.<br /><br /><span>ArcExplorer</span> looks like its heading for a December release with full functionality based on a Arc <span>backend</span> server. So any <span>GIS</span> functions such as 'line of sight' etc seem to centre on you owning a <span>ESRI</span> server license. We assumed <span>ArcExplorer</span> would be <span>GIS</span> for the masses but if its only <span>GIS</span> as long as the data providers run a Arc server (?).<br /><br />From talking to various data providers and users at the conference the <span>consensus</span> is that they also need to get some people to work on the look and feel of the user interface as at the moment its a bit clunky and does not compare well to Google Earth.<br /><br />On a <span>positive</span> note about <span>ArcExplorer</span> though its looking like it will be released without any usage <span>restrictions</span> so companies and local government can use it free of charge. This is obviously not the case with the free version of Google Earth which is only for 'personal use'.</p>
