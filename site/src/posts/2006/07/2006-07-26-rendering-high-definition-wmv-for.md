<p><img src="/assets/uploads/external/www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/andy/blogimages/PaintedHall.jpg" /><br /><br />With the arrival of new High Definition screens visualisation techniques need to be upgraded to ensure the quality is upheld when outputting to a Plasma, LCD or other High Definition capable device.<br /><br />Previously our movies were rendered in DVD quality, or 720x480 resolution. Using a custom profile it is now possible to export via the simple to use (and more importantly rapid to export) Windows Movie Maker at 1280 x 720, or 720p High Definition. <br /><br />Our first example of the <a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/andy/movies/PaintedHall.wmv">Painted Hall in Greenwich is available for download </a>and illustrates the increase in quality possible (20mb .wmv). More examples can be found at the <a href="http://www.microsoft.com/windows/windowsmedia/musicandvideo/hdvideo/contentshowcase.aspx">Windows High Definition Showcase </a>site.<br /><br />The increase in resolution does however come at the price of an increase in render times out of 3DMax for the movies. We are currently re-rendering the <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2006/07/london-movie-step-inside-panoramic.html?&tags=Movies">'Step Inside' Panorama movie of London</a> which can be seen on this blog via YouTube. Currently its looking like a 42 hour render, but as soon as its ready we will make it available for download.</p>
