As part of the <a href="http://www.bartlett.ucl.ac.uk/casa/programmes/postgraduate/mres-advanced-spatial-analysis-visualisation">MRes in Advanced Spatial Analysis and Visualisation</a>, here in <a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/">CASA</a>, we are exploring various concepts relating to urban visualisation. These include both traditional urban based modelling approaches and more abstract visualisations and simulation techniques.   <br /><br /><div style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="http://1.bp.blogspot.com/-lD7Au_CgbVI/T0lMExCG9UI/AAAAAAAACqo/p-ZKIzB0cNo/s1600/citylumion.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img border="0" height="360" src="/assets/uploads/external/1.bp.blogspot.com/-lD7Au_CgbVI/T0lMExCG9UI/AAAAAAAACqo/p-ZKIzB0cNo/s640/citylumion.jpg" width="640" /></a></div><br />Currently we are exploring applications for physics engines in urban modelling, the first step of which is a series of techniques to introduce gravity and mass to urban models. We produced <a href="http://www.digitalurban.org/2009/05/tutorial-reactor-basics-3d-max-bouncing.html">a tutorial </a>back in 2009 using 'Reactor', Autodesk no longer use this engine and have now moved onto MassFX. MassFX adds a number of new options and tweaks to the simulation which took some time to work out.
