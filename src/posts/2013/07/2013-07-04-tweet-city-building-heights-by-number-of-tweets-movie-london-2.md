What if London's buildings grew according to the amount of data they generate? Stephan Hügel (<a href="https://twitter.com/urschrei">@urschrei</a>) and Flora Roumpani (<a href="https://twitter.com/en_topia">@en_topia</a>) two of our PhD students from <a title="CASA" href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk">The Bartlett Centre for Advanced Spatial Analysis,</a> University College London, have been working on a system to read in twitter data to CityEngine and link the geo locations to building outlines.
<figure><img class=" wp-image-3458 " alt="Twitter Data linked to Building Footprints" src="/assets/uploads/2013/07/mosttwittedbuilding2-1-1024x448.jpg" width="679" height="297" /><figcaption>Twitter Data linked to Building Footprints</figcaption></figure>
The video below is the first step in an approach to visualising an urban landscape using the objects that already exist in it; instead of abstract swirling points, bars, and artefacts hovering in the sky, the buildings themselves become the medium by which the data generated in and around them are made visible:
