<p><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RZuhHTEpMzI/AAAAAAAAAB4/uYQNRcB5JZ8/s1600-h/TempleBar.jpg"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RZuhHTEpMzI/AAAAAAAAAB4/uYQNRcB5JZ8/s400/TempleBar.jpg" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5015779756758086450" border="0" /></a><br />Temple Bar is the last remaining gate of the old City of London. Built by Christopher Wren in 1672, Temple Bar divided Fleet Street and the City of London from the Strand and Westminster. It is infamous as it used to display the decapitated heads of traitors.<br /><br />It was dismantled from its original location in 1878 and re-erected ten years later in Herefordshire after being purchased by Lady Meux. In 2004 it was moved back to the City of London and now stands between Paternoster Square and St Pauls Cathedral.<br /><br />The panorama is a reprocessed version of the <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2005/09/temple-bar-and-st-pauls-cathedral.html">original Temple Bar post</a> - using High Dynamic Range processing of the RAW file it is possible to extract a noticeably wider exposure range.<br /><br />Click the image to view a larger version, <a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/andy/qt/templebar1.html">view the Quicktime panorama of Temple Bar (3.9Mb),</a>  or view other images at the <a href="http://www.flickr.com/groups/63173595@N00/">digitalurban flickr group</a>.<a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/andy/qt/templebar1.html"><br /></a></p>
