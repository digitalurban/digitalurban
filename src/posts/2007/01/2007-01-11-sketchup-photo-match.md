<a href="http://2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RaZnsZIo_LI/AAAAAAAAAD8/hZxJaSMgIYA/s1600-h/buck1.jpg"><img style="margin: 0pt 10px 10px 0pt; float: left; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RaZnsZIo_LI/AAAAAAAAAD8/hZxJaSMgIYA/s320/buck1.jpg" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5018812847110028466" border="0" /></a>  <p><span lang="EN-GB">Photomodelling has long been a specialised subject in the 3D world. Dominated by the likes of <a href="http://imagemodeler.realviz.com/products/IM/index.php?language=EN">ImageModeller</a> by Realviz and <a href="http://www.photomodeler.com/">Photomodeler</a> by Eos Systems the emphasis has always been on camera lens calibration and point matching, making it a time consuming process.<br /></span></p><p><span lang="EN-GB">Google in their release of <a href="http://www.sketchup.com/">SketchUp Pro 6</a> have now made photomodelling available to the general user in the form of their new 'Photo Match' feature. We have had chance to try it out and it significantly speeds up the modelling process for urban visualisation.<br /><br />The ability to quickly and easily set up the modelling viewpoints perspective to match a photograph allows one to simply trace a buildings outline and thus create its geometry. Once the outline is created you can then apply the image to the mesh providing instant phototexturing.</span></p>  <p><span lang="EN-GB">While its obviously not as accurate as the photogrammetric solutions it is significantly faster and easier to work with. </span></p>    <p><span lang="EN-GB"><o:p></o:p>Take a look at the <a href="http://sketchup.google.com/gsu6vtvideos.html">example movies</a> to get a closer look at the Photo Match feature.</span></p>
