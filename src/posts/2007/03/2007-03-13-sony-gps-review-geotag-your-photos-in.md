<p><a href="http://3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RfaMlcTdcUI/AAAAAAAAALA/0xOjIPeM9eo/s1600-h/GPS-CS1_with_cara.jpg"><img style="margin: 0pt 10px 10px 0pt; float: left; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RfaMlcTdcUI/AAAAAAAAALA/0xOjIPeM9eo/s200/GPS-CS1_with_cara.jpg" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5041371407768711490" border="0" /></a>On paper <a href="http://www.amazon.com/Sony-GPS-CS1-CyberShot-Digital-Cameras/dp/B000HDIYEO">Sony's GPS -CS1</a> is the perfect gadget for the urban photographer/blogger who wants to <span>geotag</span> their photographs for display in either Google Maps or Google Earth.<br /><br />The attraction of the GPS-CS1 is that it will work with any camera that can record a time and date - <span>ie</span> the majority of digital cameras. The system simply attaches to a bag or belt clip and records your track independently of the camera. Whenever you take a picture the time and date will be the same as the GPS, this allows the supplied software to link the GPS location with the photograph.<br /><br />By changing the photo's <span>EXIF</span> header to include location the images can then be easily linked to Google Maps or Google Earth via services such as <a href="http://www.flickr.com/"><span>Flickr</span></a> or <a href="http://picasa.google.com/">Picasa</a>. The unit takes a reading every 15 seconds and battery life lasts 10 hours of <span>continuous</span> use.<br /><br />On paper then the Sony GPS-CS1 is perfect so why <span>don't</span> we have any examples or Google Maps to demonstrate the output? <span>Basically</span> the unit is close to <span>useless</span> in urban areas - our tests in London only worked if we sat in an open space for a few minutes, as soon as we went to take any photographs the unit lost its signal and stopped tracking.<br /><br />While GPS coverage in urban areas is a known problem, the Sony unit seems to have a low sensitivity. With only a basic flashing LED to communicate connection it is easy to walk around town tacking photographs only to arrive back at the office to find that you <span>don't</span> have any data.<br /><br />In short if you live in an urban area then sadly the Sony GPS-CS1 turns out to be an expensive keyring, if your out of town its a great, if expensive, solution to <span>geotagging</span> photographs.</p>
