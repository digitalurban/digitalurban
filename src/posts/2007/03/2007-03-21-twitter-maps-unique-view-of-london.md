<p>As we mentioned in a our previous posts on the <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2007/03/urban-twitters.html">real time visualisation of Twitters</a> - <a href="http://twitter.com/">Twitter</a> is intriguing, part social network, part text message, part location aware it almost redefines communication in its purest sense. Limited to 140 characters and based around a simple text box - a text box, asking "What are you doing?" it is difficult to understand at first..<br /><br />The <span>realtime</span> visualisation of Twitter is an interesting visualisation - but in terms of the city then <a href="http://twittermap.com/maps">Twitter Maps</a> lets you get down to street level to view people posting their 'Tweets' (that's Twitter language for <span>messages</span>). The <span>screengrab</span> below is the Twitter Map zoomed into London and there are a surprising number of people Twittering this morning:<br /><br /><a href="http://1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RgEENPxl2CI/AAAAAAAAAMg/EupPgMLaGu8/s1600-h/twitterlondon.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RgEENPxl2CI/AAAAAAAAAMg/EupPgMLaGu8/s400/twitterlondon.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5044317683251009570" border="0" /></a><br />Twitters are simply about what people are doing - for example in the image above <span>Redmedicine</span> posted 'Finishing breakfast, mopping up a leaky cappuccino and staring down the barrel of   another wonderful day.  But the sky is blue!' Simple yet addictive and by linking it to a mapping interface it provides a unique visualisation about what people are doing in the city right now.<br /><br />Twitter is tipped to be the next important social network on the web, indeed <a href="http://money.cnn.com/magazines/business2/business2_archive/2007/02/01/8398977/index.htm">CNN Money</a> state that '<span style="font-style: italic;">the free service, which combines the instant voyeurism of personal blogs with the brevity of text messages, appears to be catching fire, doubling its user base every month. It had 10,000 users by December and expects to hit 100,000 by April. Users post pithy updates on their lives (in no more than 160 characters) via the website, their <span>IM</span> clients, or their mobile devices. These posts can be sent just to their circle of friends or, if the user allows it, they can be added to <span>Twitter's</span> public time line. (A free desktop widget called <span>Twidget</span> also features the updates.)</span><br /><br />You can <a href="http://twitter.com/digitalurban">join our Twitter network</a> and take part in the emerging Twitter <span>phenomoenon</span>. At the moment on our side bar to the right are our current Tweets, once we get a network in place we will be able to display other peoples Tweets, which would be an intriguing insight into the readers of Digital Urban.<br /><br />We will be posting more applications for Twitter networks over the coming weeks.</p>
