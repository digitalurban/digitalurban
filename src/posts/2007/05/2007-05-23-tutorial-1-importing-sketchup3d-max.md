<p>This tutorial is aimed at providing a clear and easy to follow path through the often confusing route of using the Oblivion Game Engine for visualistion.<br /><br />Oblivion is an impressive engine and once you have completed the tutorial it should become second nature to go from <span><span><span>SketchUp</span></span></span>/3D Max to real-time visualisation with dynamic lighting and weather effects.<br /><br />Due to the nature of the tutorial we have divided it into two parts. The first is aimed at setting up the level editor, known as the Elder Scrolls Construction set, and creating your first world. The second part provides a walk through from <span><span><span>SketchUp</span></span></span> and 3D Max into the environment you will of created by the end of this post. More information and guides can be found at the <a href="http://cs.elderscrolls.com/constwiki/index.php/Main_Page">Elder Scrolls Wiki</a>.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Tutorial 1</span><br /><br />Requirements:<br /><br />1) A copy of Oblivion (you can <a href="http://astore.amazon.com/digiurba-20/104-7240808-6091906?%5Fencoding=UTF8&node=2">purchase it direct from our Amazon Store</a> or any good games outlet)<br /><br />2) A copy of the <a href="http://www.elderscrolls.com/downloads/updates_utilities.htm">Elder Scrolls Construction Kit</a> (free )<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Setting Up</span><br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 1</span><br /><br />Launch the Elder Scrolls Construction Kit from the Bethesda <span><span><span>Softworks</span></span></span> link in your Windows 'All Programs' list. Your screen will look similar to the image below.<br /><br /><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQvQVXQfmI/AAAAAAAAAVw/y-Tf4JUPrP4/s1600-h/Capture1.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQvQVXQfmI/AAAAAAAAAVw/y-Tf4JUPrP4/s320/Capture1.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5067727438357757538" border="0" /></a><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 2</span><br /><br />Select 'File' then 'Data' and load Oblivion.<span><span><span>esm</span></span></span> from the list. This loads all of the object sets and the environments from the game into the construction set.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Creating Your World</span><br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 1</span><br /><br />Select 'Worlds' then 'World Spaces' from the top menu bar.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 2</span><br /><br />Right Click on any of the World Names in the list under 'Editor ID', Select 'New'<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 3</span><br /><br />Name your world in the dialogue box - we used <span><span>DigitalUrban</span></span>. Select the following options as per the image below (click for larger version). In short - Sharable Data 'Clear Climate', Usable Dimensions 'X=3 Y=3', Tick Oblivion World Space and Small World.<br /><br /><a href="http://1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQw3lXQfnI/AAAAAAAAAV4/fqpTd9-JoxA/s1600-h/Capture2.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQw3lXQfnI/AAAAAAAAAV4/fqpTd9-JoxA/s320/Capture2.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5067729212179250802" border="0" /></a>Once the options are selected, Click 'OK'.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 4</span><br /><br />Your World is now named and created ready to select as a World Space. Click the drop down box in the Cell View/World Space and select your World - in our case <span><span>DigitalUrban</span></span> as pictured below.<br /><br /><a href="http://3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQysFXQfoI/AAAAAAAAAWA/7KZQpH16MBY/s1600-h/Capture3.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQysFXQfoI/AAAAAAAAAWA/7KZQpH16MBY/s320/Capture3.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5067731213634010754" border="0" /></a><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 5</span><br /><br />Double Click 'Wilderness'. You will now have a list of cells, all named Wilderness and a purple screen in the soon to become 3D view:<br /><br /><a href="http://3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQzwFXQfpI/AAAAAAAAAWI/bh6Sn3OqISA/s1600-h/Capture4.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQzwFXQfpI/AAAAAAAAAWI/bh6Sn3OqISA/s320/Capture4.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5067732381865115282" border="0" /></a><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Making a Landscape to Build On<br /><br /></span>We now have a defined but empty world, to build or import objects we need a flat plain of land. Oblivion is able to display complex landscapes, however for our application we simply require space to visualise.<span style="font-weight: bold;"><br /></span><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 1</span><br /><br />Select 'World' then '<span><span><span>Heightmap</span></span></span> Editing' and your world name from the pop up dialogue box - this opens to <span><span><span>Heightmap</span></span></span> Editing Tool.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 2</span><br /><br />To create a flat plain set both 'Frequency' and 'Amplitude' to 1 and 'Base Offset' to 5000. Click 'New Seed' and then Click 'Generate'. The construction set will now create an even height map according to the height indicated in the Base Offset - in our case 5000 and a resulting blue fill.<br /><br />To create a mountainous landscape change the numbers in Frequency and Amplitude. Your <span><span><span>Heightmap</span></span></span> should now look as per the image below:<br /><br /><br /><a href="http://2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQ_z1XQfqI/AAAAAAAAAWQ/xRbNtLCV68M/s1600-h/Capture5.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlQ_z1XQfqI/AAAAAAAAAWQ/xRbNtLCV68M/s320/Capture5.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5067745640429158050" border="0" /></a>Click 'Save', wait for the editor to complete the process and then close the height editor - your terrain to build on is now complete.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Your First Object<br /><br /></span>We now need to carry out a few steps to make sure we know what 'cell' we are building in and to ensure that the relevant cell is selected.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 1</span><br /><br />Rename the first cell on the list  - currently called Wilderness - Click once on 'Wilderness' and wait, you will now be able to rename the cell, we used the name 'Cell1'<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 2</span><br /><br />We need to make sure the 3D window is dark green and not purple - if it is purple double click on any cell and then double click back on Cell1. This will make sure it is selected.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 3</span><br /><br />The world is currently on the dark side - Click the '<span><span><span>Lightbulb</span></span></span>' icon in the top bar. The 3D window will now turn a lighter green highlighting the grass in Cell1.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 4</span><br /><br />In the Object Window select 'World Objects' and the 'Trees' you will see a list of trees that you can add to your world. Select any tree from the list and 'Click and Drag' to the 3D Window. We selected 'Tree <span><span><span>EastHemlock</span></span></span>'. Your tree will now be floating above the world as pictured below:<br /><br /><br /><a href="http://2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlRDh1XQfrI/AAAAAAAAAWY/2YWpO4WopzM/s1600-h/Capture6.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlRDh1XQfrI/AAAAAAAAAWY/2YWpO4WopzM/s320/Capture6.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5067749729238023858" border="0" /></a><br />To make the tree fall to the ground 'Press F'. You can now take some time to get used to the 3D navig<br />
ation in the window. Use the Mouse Scroll Wheel to Zoom and Out, Hold Down Shift and Move the Mouse to Rotate. Click on the Tree Icon in the Cells List to jump back to an overview of the object.<br /><br />Any object from the 'Static' objects list can also be dragged into the window. This is how we are going to add our import from Sketchup/3D Max in part 2.<br /><br />Save your world by clicking the 'Save Icon' and give your world a logical name, we used <span><span><span>digitalurban</span></span></span>.esp<br /><br /><span><span>Constuction</span></span> of your first world is now complete, close the Elder Scrolls Construction Set.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Viewing Your World in Oblivion</span><br /><br />There are now just a couple of steps to take to enable Oblivion to load up your new world rather than the default game.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 1</span><br /><br />Go to the folder - 'My Documents'/'My Games'/'Oblivion' and open Oblivion.<span><span><span>ini</span></span></span> in either Notepad or <span><span><span>Wordpad</span></span></span>. We are now going to edit the file to ensure Oblivion loads our new world.<br /><br />Make three changes to the Oblivion.<span><span><span>ini</span></span></span> file - add your Starting Cell name, in our case Cell1, your Starting World name and the name of your file in <span><span><span>STestFile</span></span></span>10=, in our case again <span><span><span>digitalurban</span></span></span>.<br /><br /><br /><a href="http://1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlRHSlXQfsI/AAAAAAAAAWg/HcsU6cN-fus/s1600-h/Capture7.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlRHSlXQfsI/AAAAAAAAAWg/HcsU6cN-fus/s320/Capture7.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5067753865291529922" border="0" /></a><br />Save this file.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 2<br /><br /></span>Launch Oblivion - you are now ready to view your world. Select 'Data Files' from the Oblivion start screen. A menu will appear allowing you to load your new world. Select 'Oblivion.esm' and 'YourWorldName.esp' - ours is DigitalUrban.esp. Click 'OK' and now Click 'Play'.<br /><br /><br /><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlVp8FXQftI/AAAAAAAAAWo/pqfpEUIsZW4/s1600-h/Capture8.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RlVp8FXQftI/AAAAAAAAAWo/pqfpEUIsZW4/s320/Capture8.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5068073436628156114" border="0" /></a><br /><br />The <span><span><span>YouTube</span></span></span> Movie below demonstrates our sample world created by following the above steps:<br /><br /><object height="350" width="425"><param name="movie" value="http://www.youtube.com/v/qM_X6nonenw"><embed src="http://www.youtube.com/v/qM_X6nonenw" type="application/x-shockwave-flash" height="350" width="425"></embed></object><br /><br />Note that when the world first loads the graphics are flicking and the sky is displaying errors.<br /><br />This is easily fixed by:<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 1</span><br /><br />Press the key next to 1 on most keyboards labelled '¬' or '~' this allows console commands to be entered. Type 'TS' and then 'Press Enter' now repeat and you will see that the sky is now correctly turned on. There are a number of commands you can use in this mode to turn functions such as Flying, God Mode and Weather Effects on and off,  see <a href="http://uk.gamespot.com/pc/rpg/theelderscrollsivoblivion/hints.html"><span><span><span>GameSpots</span></span></span> guide</a> for a full list.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 2</span><br /><br />Press 'T' to allow you to advance in time - towards daylight, select 7 hours.<br /><br />Your world is now complete and navigable within the Oblivion Engine. Use W/S to move <span><span>forwards</span></span> and backwards, A/D to move left and right. Press 'R' for a third person viewpoint.<br /><br />In Tutorial 2, <span>available</span> shortly, we will export an object from <span><span><span>SketchUp</span></span></span> and 3D Max directly into our new world. You can view some SketchUp/3D Max examples so far from our <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2007/05/oblivion-sketchup3d-max-import-examples.html">previous post</a>.<br /><br /><a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2007/06/tutorial-importing-sketchup3d-max-into.html">Part 2 of this tutorial can be found here</a>.<br /><br />See also <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2007/05/oblivion-step-inside-panoramas-sample.html">3D Max Immersive Panoramic Spheres in Oblivion</a><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;"></span></p>
