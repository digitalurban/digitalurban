<p><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RtWi3sCiTpI/AAAAAAAAAng/8auhMWUMAUY/s1600-h/Capture.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RtWi3sCiTpI/AAAAAAAAAng/8auhMWUMAUY/s400/Capture.JPG" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5104164830292627090" border="0" /></a>Our section in Second Life, kindly donated by <a href="http://www.nature.com/">Nature</a>, is focused on communicating city space. As such the image of San Francisco from Virtual Earth, created as part of our <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2007/08/how-to-create-aerial-panoramas-from.html">tutorial on Virtual Earth panoramas</a>, is a natural choice to include within the environment.<br /><br />Central to this is the need to ensure that the original visual fidelity is withheld. In Second Life the maximum texture size is 1024x1024 pixels resulting in a 6 to 1 compression of our panoramic image. There is however a simple way to match the original resolution as closely as possible by splitting the image up into tiles in <span>Photoshop</span>, as pictured below:<br /><br /><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RtWiHsCiTnI/AAAAAAAAAnQ/UOeBEzVWT14/s1600-h/sliced.jpg"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RtWiHsCiTnI/AAAAAAAAAnQ/UOeBEzVWT14/s400/sliced.jpg" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5104164005658906226" border="0" /></a>By noting down the pixel size of each tile a 'prim' in Second Life can be created at the required aspect ratio - thus to recreate the whole image 16 '<span>prims</span>' are required aligned using the grid snap option.<br /><br />This quick and easy method results in a visually impressive image wall - as pictured below (click for a larger version):<br /><br /><a href="http://2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RtWiwMCiToI/AAAAAAAAAnY/0iOxE9Ikric/s1600-h/Capture3.JPG"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px;
