<p>The majority of the posts in the blog include self captured movies so we thought we would put together a simple tutorial to answer all the questions we get on how they are made.<br />
Creating an engaging movie takes three elements - a high and constant frame rate of capture, an interesting flight path and a suitable sound track. With these in mind you can make a movie from either Google Earth or Microsoft's Virtual Earth in under 15 minutes.<br />
<span style="font-weight: bold;">Requirements</span><br />
1) <span>FRAPS</span> - <span>FRAPS</span> is without doubt the best way to capture what you see on the screen in <span>realtime</span> from either Google, Virtual Earth or any game/virtual environment that uses <span>DirectX</span> or Open <span>GL</span> - this includes Second Life.<br />
<a href="http://www.fraps.com/"><span>Fraps</span></a> is available for $37 and is honestly one of the most useful pieces of software we have bought.<br />
2) <a href="http://www.3dconnexion.com/products/3a1d.php">Space Navigator</a> - If <span>Fraps</span> is the most useful software we have purchased recently then the Space Navigator is the best, and cheapest, hardware. Priced at $59 , £39 or 59 Euros the Personal Edition of the Space Navigator allows you to create smooth flight paths in both Google and Virtual Earth, it also works in many other 3D packages.<br />
The <span>YouTube</span> movie below provides a further insight for those new to the Space Navigator:<br />

<span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 1</span><br />
Load both <span>FRAPS</span> and your program from which to capture - in our case Google Earth as pictured below:<br />
<a href="http://1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RtfjlcCiTuI/AAAAAAAAAoI/WGCUVXirM8Y/s1600-h/screen1.jpg"><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5104798934969241314" style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/RtfjlcCiTuI/AAAAAAAAAoI/WGCUVXirM8Y/s400/screen1.jpg" alt="" border="0" /></a><br />
<span>FRAPS</span> uses a <span>hotkey</span> to start recording - press your assigned <span>HotKey</span> (ours is F12) and start to fly around your scene. It is best to try out your flight path a couple of times before recording to ensure it is smooth and the required textures are preloaded.<br />
Press your <span>HotKey</span> again to stop the movie - this will give you phase one of the movie as per below:<br />
<center><object width="425" height="350" classid="clsid:d27cdb6e-ae6d-11cf-96b8-444553540000" codebase="http://download.macromedia.com/pub/shockwave/cabs/flash/swflash.cab#version=6,0,40,0"><param name="src" value="http://www.youtube.com/v/svATJTzt4bY" /><embed width="425" height="350" type="application/x-shockwave-flash" src="http://www.youtube.com/v/svATJTzt4bY" /></object></center>The movie above is not very engaging, if it was used in an educational context the users attention would be waning by the time the flight path reaches the glacier.<br />
As such the movie requires context, using the Space Navigator the movie was recorded to roughly simulate a helicopter flight, adding sound effects and a music track is the second part of the movie creation.<br />
<span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 2</span><br />
<span>We use Windows Movie Maker which although a low end video editing package it is suitable for such movies.</span><br />
<a href="http://3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/Rtfm78CiTvI/AAAAAAAAAoQ/wlVzw5sZ3IE/s1600-h/screen2.jpg"><img id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5104802620051181298" style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer;" src="/assets/uploads/external/3.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/Rtfm78CiTvI/AAAAAAAAAoQ/wlVzw5sZ3IE/s400/screen2.jpg" alt="" border="0" /></a>To add to the location we used the <a href="http://www.fudgefactor.org/2005/05/using-sound-in-horror-games.html">sound of a helicopter in flight from here</a> which is subsequently imported into Movie Maker and dragged into the movie clip bar as many times as required to play throughout the clip. The sound trails off towards the end so to ensure a continuous sound effect each clip is overlapped - as pictured above.<br />
Movie Maker only allows one audio track to be added - as we mentioned its a low end editing tool - as such the movie needs to be published and <span>reimported</span> if another sound track is to be added.<br />
<span style="font-weight: bold;">Step 3</span><br />
<span>Publish the movie as an .<span>avi</span> to minimise any loss of quality and <span>reimport</span> the saved movie, this now allows you to add a new soundtrack.</span><br />
The choice of soundtrack is dependent on both the subject matter and the speed of the movie, for mountain type scenes a general ambient atmosphere is preferable. Whatever your choice of genre a good place to download music for inclusion in movies is <a href="http://www.mp3unsigned.com/">http://www.mp3unsigned.com</a><br />
The final step is to add in your chosen soundtrack and republish the movie. We normally publish as a .<span>wmv</span>, DVD quality and then upload to <span>YouTube</span> for inclusion in the blog.<br />

The quality of <span>Google's</span> <span> imagery for the Swiss Alps is</span><span> <span>approaching</span> realism, making it perfect for educational use in physical geography.</span><br />
We wish we had Google Earth and movies as per above when learning about Glaciers in school...<br />
