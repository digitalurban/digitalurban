<p>At the moment we are mid-writing with various,  papers, technical reports and book chapters all seemingly with the deadline of next week. As such and while looking back through previous papers and grants we have identified the 10 phases and buzz words that are either on the rise or on their way down.<b><br /></b><br /><br /><b>Buzz Words on the up... </b><br /><br /><b>GeoCloud</b> - geographic data and visualisation tools via cloud computing, we used it in a paper last year and it still feels timely.<br /><br /><b>Digital Recursion</b> - the activity of representing and accessing digital media which is nested in some form within computer networks. A phrase by Mike Batty, again in a joint paper from last year (<a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/search/label/Publications">see our publications page</a>), he has a tendency to come up with catchy terms.<br /><br /><b>Web 3.0</b> - although annoying to many after the over use of Web 2.0, Web 3.0 is arguably  read/write/execute with the operating system and the web being one and the same.<br /><br /><b>Volunteered Geographic Information (VGI)</b> - is the harnessing of tools to create, assemble, and disseminate  geographic data provided voluntarily by individuals (Goodchild, 2007). Not a new term by any means but still a good one to use in any paper or grant involving geographic information. Indeed its one of the those phases you wish you had come up with yourself.<sup><br /></sup><br /><br /><b>Steady </b><br /><br /><b>Mirror Worlds</b> - representations of the real world in scaled down simplified form that were originally pictured as working in parallel to the reality itself but with strong interaction both ways between reality and it mirror. The term was first popularized by David Gerlernter.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Social Shaping</span> - although not a new term by any means it  crops up a lot in papers and grant applications at the moment. In short  the term can be linked back to MacKenzie and Wajcman's 1985 publication '<a href="http://www.amazon.com/Social-Shaping-Technology-Donald-MacKenzie/dp/0335199135">The  Social Shaping of Technology</a>' where they state that the  characteristics of a society play a major part in deciding which  technologies are adopted.<br /><br />With the rise of browser technologies the concepts behind social shaping provide an interesting  take on which tech comes to the forefront and we would argue their  ever shortening lifespan.<br /><br /><br /><b>Buzz words on the way down...</b><br /><br /><b>Digital</b> -   technology that uses discrete (discontinuous) values. By contrast,  non-digital (or <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_signal" title="Analog signal">analog</a>) systems use a <a href="http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuum_%28mathematics%29" title="Continuum (mathematics)">continuous</a> range  of values to represent information. Slightly worrying as that's the name of the blog, it just feels a bit 90's...<br /><br /><b>Neogeography</b>  - a diverse set of practices that operate outside, or alongside, or in a manner of, the practices of professional geographers. As we mentioned in a <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2010/02/come-in-neogeography-your-time-is-up.html">previous post</a>, that was 2006-2009, its time to move on.<br /><br /><b>Far Down -</b><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;"> </span><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;"> </span><b>The Grid</b> - increasingly being replaced in papers  by mentioning Web Based Services, which it could be argued can also be  seen as The Cloud. <a href="http://e-science.ox.ac.uk/what/">The Oxford  e-Science Centre</a> define The Grids as:<br /><blockquote>The name that  describes the next significant development in Internet computing. A term  first coined in the mid '90s to describe a vision for a distributed  computing infrastructure for advanced science projects, the Grid was  first properly explained by Ian Foster and Carl Kesselman in their book <a href="http://www.blogger.com/he%20Grid%3A%20Blueprint%20for%20a%20New%20Computing%20Infrastructure"><i>The  Grid: Blueprint for a New Computing Infrastructure.</i></a></blockquote>The Grid is currently lost in the <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2008/06/grid-computing-waves-of-hype-and.html">trough of disillusionment</a> and all those hours sat at conferences talking about it feel a bit wasted.<br /><br /><span style="font-weight: bold;">Web 2.0</span> - the term Web 2.0  has been around since 2004 and is still at the forefront of many  academic discussions on the future of technology.
