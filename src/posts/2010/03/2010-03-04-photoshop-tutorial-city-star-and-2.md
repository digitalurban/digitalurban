Last week we covered creating 'day trails' in Photoshop using the technique in Astrophotography known as 'star trails'. Today we take the same technique and use it to create a view of city activity at night.<br /><br /><a href="http://1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/S4-UeWg4qNI/AAAAAAAACVY/y2muKeYtBjg/s1600-h/nightlights.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img src="/assets/uploads/external/1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/S4-UeWg4qNI/AAAAAAAACVY/y2muKeYtBjg/s640/nightlights.jpg" border="0" height="160" width="600" /></a><b><br /><br />You will need:</b><br /><div style="text-align: center;"><br /></div>1  x Timelapse System, you can use a simple webcam as per our previous <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2008/11/tutorial-torch-webcam-hd-timelapse.html">Tutorial:   Torch + Webcam = HD Timelapse System</a> a DSLR such as the <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2009/12/canon-g9-timelapse-1080p-with-chdk.html">Canon  G9 with CHDK </a>, a <a href="http://digitalurban.blogspot.com/2010/02/timelapse-on-iphone-look-at-free.html">iPhone  with the free  Gorrilacam app</a> or any camera that can take photos at  regular intervals. We used a Go Pro HD camera in timelapse mode, taking a picture every 5 seconds.<br /><br />1 x Copy of Photoshop, you can <a href="http://www.adobe.com/products/photoshop/compare/">download a 30  day trial</a>.<br /><br />1 x Photoshop <a href="http://www.schursastrophotography.com/software/photoshop/Startrails.atn">Stacking  Action</a> (thanks to <a href="http://www.schursastrophotography.com/index.html">Deep Space  Astrophotography</a>)<br /><br />Time Taken, 4 to 12 hours to capture, 2 to 6 hours to process.<br /><br /><b>Setting Up</b><br /><br />The concept is simple, set  up your camera, webcam or iphone at a suitable location, and capture an  image at regular intervals, for our example we captured an image every 5  seconds pointing at the skyline of London. Capturing an image at least every 5 seconds is vital for star/aircraft trails as it allows for closer spacing between the lights in the final image.<br /><br /><div style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="http://2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/S4-UzFkENgI/AAAAAAAACVc/ycg4PX6dCuQ/s1600-h/7240.JPG" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img src="/assets/uploads/external/2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/S4-UzFkENgI/AAAAAAAACVc/ycg4PX6dCuQ/s640/7240.JPG" border="0" height="480" width="640" /></a></div><br /><div style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/S4JZcIk-_gI/AAAAAAAACU0/15Mo6fmbCfE/s1600-h/nonstacked.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><br /></a></div>We left the camera  running for approximately 12 hours capturing 8000+ images, saved into a folder on our  computer. Ours captured covered both day and night time, resulting in the following timelapse:<br /><br /><br /><br /><center><object height="345" width="600"><param name="movie" value="http://www.youtube.com/v/QPUYKg30SSA&hl=en_US&fs=1&"><param name="allowFullScreen" value="true"><param name="allowscriptaccess" value="always"><embed src="http://www.youtube.com/v/QPUYKg30SSA&hl=en_US&fs=1&" type="application/x-shockwave-flash" allowscriptaccess="always" allowfullscreen="true" height="345" width="600"></embed></object></center><br /><br />The next step is to open up photoshop, chose the images you want to use,  and start stacking.<br /><br /><b>Image Stacking in Photoshop</b><br /><br />The images  will be stacked onto of an intially blank image via a simple automated  action:<br /><br />1) Create a new blank black image the same size are your  captured photographs.<br /><br />2) Load the action into the action windows  in Photoshop and load the action Startrails.atn.<br /><br /><div style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="http://2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/S4Jc0yTcnjI/AAAAAAAACU4/JHAhRr80DMc/s1600-h/loadaction.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img src="/assets/uploads/external/2.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/S4Jc0yTcnjI/AAAAAAAACU4/JHAhRr80DMc/s400/loadaction.jpg" border="0" height="327" width="400" /></a></div><br />3)  In Photoshop  click 'File', 'Automate' and 'Batch'. Select the action you have just  loaded and choose your directory with the images as source and make sure  you select 'None' for the output directory.<br /><br />Click 'Ok' and leave  it running, our Mac laptop took around an 2 hours to stack the images -  resulting in the  Start/Aircraft Trail' below:<br /><br /><div style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/S4-V8F8y5mI/AAAAAAAACVk/ejX5nUaq4uU/s1600-h/CityLightsGoPro.jpg" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/S4-V8F8y5mI/AAAAAAAACVk/ejX5nUaq4uU/s640/CityLightsGoPro.jpg" border="0" height="480" width="600" /></a></div><br />The line across the centre is a star and the bright line on the left is the moon coming into shot. The rest of the lights are aircraft in the sky above London.<br /><br />You can view higher resolution versions via <a href="http://www.flickr.com/photos/84334252@N00/">our Flickr Photostream</a>.<br /><div style="clear: both; text-align: center;"></div>
