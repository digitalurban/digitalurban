<p>Summer seems to be a good time of year to dig out those movies that we have liked over our time on digital urban so far. First off we are going to look at our own movies (its slightly indulgent) that in the 1500 odd posts often get lost, we will run through why they were made, the software involved and their place in the bigger picture.<br /><br />Full post with movies after the break below:<br /><br /><a name='more'></a><br /><br /><br /><b>No. 5: Growing the Tube Network </b><br /><br /><br /><br /><center><object height="360" width="640"><param name="allowfullscreen" value="true"><param name="allowscriptaccess" value="always"><param name="movie" value="http://vimeo.com/moogaloop.swf?clip_id=6910245&server=vimeo.com&show_title=0&show_byline=0&show_portrait=0&color=ffffff&fullscreen=1"><embed src="http://vimeo.com/moogaloop.swf?clip_id=6910245&server=vimeo.com&show_title=0&show_byline=0&show_portrait=0&color=ffffff&fullscreen=1" type="application/x-shockwave-flash" allowfullscreen="true" allowscriptaccess="always" height="360" width="640"></embed></object></center><br /><br />The clip was produced in 3D Max, purely as a way to explore the rapid creation of transport networks for visualisation. It was easy to make - simply import a PDF of a transport network into a package such as illustrator and save as .dwg. This allows you to import into 3D Max and then the lines can be converted to 'tubes'  - the network can be built up in under an hours work. Making the stations and naming the lines took a little more time but the result is interesting. The render has been used on the cover of a couple of books, been hung on the walls in the London Transport Museum and is the subject of future development with real network flows.<br /><br /><br /><b>No. 4:  Reactor and Particles in the City</b><br /><br />Number 4 is a slight cheat as it contains two movies, firstly Reactor:<br /><br /><br /><br /><br /><center><object height="360" width="640"><param name="allowfullscreen" value="true"><param name="allowscriptaccess" value="always"><param name="movie" value="http://vimeo.com/moogaloop.swf?clip_id=2213162&server=vimeo.com&show_title=0&show_byline=0&show_portrait=0&color=ffffff&fullscreen=1"><embed src="http://vimeo.com/moogaloop.swf?clip_id=2213162&server=vimeo.com&show_title=0&show_byline=0&show_portrait=0&color=ffffff&fullscreen=1" type="application/x-shockwave-flash" allowfullscreen="true" allowscriptaccess="always" height="360" width="640"></embed></object></center><br /><br />Secondly, Particles:<br /><br /><br /><br /><center><object height="361" width="640"><param name="allowfullscreen" value="true"><param name="allowscriptaccess" value="always"><param name="movie" value="http://vimeo.com/moogaloop.swf?clip_id=2203034&server=vimeo.com&show_title=0&show_byline=0&show_portrait=0&color=ffffff&fullscreen=1"><embed src="http://vimeo.com/moogaloop.swf?clip_id=2203034&server=vimeo.com&show_title=0&show_byline=0&show_portrait=0&color=ffffff&fullscreen=1" type="application/x-shockwave-flash" allowfullscreen="true" allowscriptaccess="always" height="361" width="640"></embed></object></center><br /><br />The movies were produced as part of exploring how 3D Max can be used for agent based modelling, we wrote a <a href="http://www.digitalurban.org/2009/05/tutorial-reactor-basics-3d-max-bouncing.html">Complete tutorial on Reactor and Bouncing Balls in the City</a>. The work is currently being imported into the Unity engine as part of our work on NeISS. Talking of which:<br /><br /><b>No. 3: Unity</b><br /><br />Unity is our current engine of choice, simply for its ease of use for importing models and producing quality output that can run either online, via iPhones or on an iPad.  all aspects of our work. We hope a few of them have provided some inspiration over the years or via <a href="http://www.digitalurban.org/2007/09/digital-urban-tutorials.html">our tutorials</a> we have managed to show some useful tips and techniques.</p>
