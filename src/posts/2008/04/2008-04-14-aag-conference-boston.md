<p><a href="http://www.flyawaycafe.com/wp-content/uploads/2007/07/downtown-boston-skyline-thumb.jpg"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer; width: 248px; height: 186px;" src="http://www.flyawaycafe.com/wp-content/uploads/2007/07/downtown-boston-skyline-thumb.jpg" alt="" border="0" /></a><strong><span style="font-weight: normal;">We are heading off to the <a href="http://communicate.aag.org/eseries/aag_org/program/AbstractDetail.cfm?AbstractID=18748">Association of American Geographers (<span>AAG</span>) Annual Conference</a> in Boston on Wednesday. The <span>AAG</span> is always a good place to touch base with the latest research, we are presenting a paper on Saturday for those interested, the full details are below:<br /></span><br />Abstract Title:<br /><em>Web 2.0 and <span>CyberInfrastructure</span>: <span>GeoVue</span> Tools and Virtual Worlds for Geographic Visualisation</em><br /><br />is part of the Paper Session:<br /><u><a href="http://communicate.aag.org/eseries/aag_org/program/SessionDetail.cfm?SessionID=6330">Build it, <span>Mapt</span> it, Web it</a></u><br /><br />scheduled on Saturday, 4/19/08 at 10:10 AM.<br /></strong><br /><strong>Author(s):</strong><br />Andrew Hudson-Smith, Dr* - University College London<br /><br /><strong>Abstract:<br /><br /></strong> Web 2.0, specifically The Cloud, <span>GeoWeb</span> and <span>Wikitecture</span> are revolutionizing the way in which we present, share and analyze geographic data.  In this paper we outline and provide working examples using our suite of tools which are detailed below, aimed at developing new applications of <span>GIS</span> and related technologies.<br /><br /><span>GeoVUE</span> is one of seven nodes in the National Centre for e-Social Science whose mission it is to develop web-based technologies for the social and geographical sciences. The Node, based at the Centre for Advanced Spatial Analysis, University College London has developed a suite of free software allowing quick and easy visualization of geographic data in systems such as Google Maps, Google Earth and Second Life. These tools address two issues, firstly  that spatial data is still inherently difficult to share and visualize for the non-<span>GIS</span> trained academic or professional and secondly that a geographic data social network has the potential to dramatically open up data sources for both the public and professional geographer.<br /><br />With our applications of <span>GMap</span> Creator, Image Cutter, <span>PhotoOverlay</span> Creator, <span>GEarth</span> Creator and finally <span>MapTube</span>,  we detail ways to intelligently visualize and share spatial data. This paper concludes with detailing usage and outreach as well as an insight into how such tools are already providing a significant impact to the outreach of geographic information.<br /><br /><strong>Keywords:</strong><br /><br />           <a href="http://communicate.aag.org/eseries/aag_org/program/SearchKeywords.cfm?Keyword=google%20earth"><span>google</span> earth</a>,<a href="http://communicate.aag.org/eseries/aag_org/program/SearchKeywords.cfm?Keyword=web%202.0">web 2.0</a>,<a href="http://communicate.aag.org/eseries/aag_org/program/SearchKeywords.cfm?Keyword=second%20life"> second life</a>,<a href="http://communicate.aag.org/eseries/aag_org/program/SearchKeywords.cfm?Keyword=visualisation"> visualisation</a>,<a href="http://communicate.aag.org/eseries/aag_org/program/SearchKeywords.cfm?Keyword=google%20maps"> <span>google</span> maps</a><br /><br />Fingers crossed for good weather as we are packing our panorama kit, all being well our previously delayed (due to fog in <span>Hong</span> Kong)  <span>HDR</span> tutorial should go online next week.</p>
