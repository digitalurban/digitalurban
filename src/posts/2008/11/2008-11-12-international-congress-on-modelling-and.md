<p><a href="http://1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/SRqy0-SxZLI/AAAAAAAAB1o/_ZAk772FScs/s1600-h/imacs.png"><img style="margin: 0px auto 10px; display: block; text-align: center; cursor: pointer; width: 400px; height: 119px;" src="/assets/uploads/external/1.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/SRqy0-SxZLI/AAAAAAAAB1o/_ZAk772FScs/s400/imacs.png" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5267719337307890866" border="0" /></a><br />The International Congress on Modelling and Simulation 2009 is in Cairns, Australia.<br /><br />The congress brings together a broad spectrum of scientists who apply various mathematical modelling, simulation, statistical, engineering, spatial and computer science methodologies and skills in solving practical problems coming from a wide range of endeavours including environmental and global change modelling, water resources management, health care, biological simulation and engineering.<br /><br />Of specific interest is the call for papers on Landscape Visualisation in Support of Management Decisions.<br /><br />There are many tools and techniques available for visualising landscape processes and condition (existing and future). Visualisation can provide a window to complex biophysical and socio-economic models and can assist communities, industry and decision-makers better understand landscape condition and the likely impact of land management decisions.<br /><br />The session will provide a forum to report and critique the latest developments and applications in landscape visualisation. Papers that describe the application and/or recent developments of landscape visualisation technologies such as digital globes, online collaborative environments, computer gaming engines, virtual reality and augmented reality are encouraged.<br /><br />Possible areas of interest include: <span style="font-style: italic; font-weight: bold;">visualisation tools and techniques for exploring both natural and built landscapes</span> under a changing climate, visualisation <span style="font-style: italic; font-weight: bold;">as a front end for complex models</span>, and studies which evaluate the usability and usefulness of visualisation technologies for spatial decision support.<br /><br />The session will be run in conjunction with ISPRS Working Groups II 6 - Geographical Visualization and Virtual Reality and WG II 7 - Spatial Decision Support Systems:<br /><br /><a href="http://www.mssanz.org.au/modsim09/page26.html">http://www.mssanz.org.au/modsim09/page26.html</a><br /><br />For more details email Dr Chris Pettit (<a href="http://www.blogger.com/Christopher.Pettit@dpi.vic.gov.au">Christopher.Pettit@dpi.vic.gov.au</a>)<br /><br />Deadline for abstracts is 28 November 2008.</p>
