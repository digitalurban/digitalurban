<p><a href="http://4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/R7Al_N8ZrEI/AAAAAAAABDk/L-TXBF0f5nk/s1600-h/covermed.gif"><img style="margin: 0pt 0pt 10px 10px; float: right; cursor: pointer; width: 163px; height: 220px;" src="/assets/uploads/external/4.bp.blogspot.com/_ADwvfqkxChw/R7Al_N8ZrEI/AAAAAAAABDk/L-TXBF0f5nk/s320/covermed.gif" alt="" id="BLOGGER_PHOTO_ID_5165670540598946882" border="0" /></a>By 2030 the number of urban dwellers will have exploded to 4.8 billion people, roughly 60 percent of the projected world population, whereas only 13 percent lived in cities in 1900. In its 8 February 2008 issue, <a href="http://www.sciencemag.org/">Science</a> explores the ramifications of urban transformation. News articles offer an on-the-ground look at how cities are tackling specific problems from poverty and sanitation to traffic jams.<br /><br />Reviews and Perspectives examine how cities take shape and the impacts of urbanization on the environment, human health, economic growth, and the demographics of the developing world.<br /><br />Mike Batty, director of our Lab here in <a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/">CASA</a>, has a paper in and also appears as part of the  related <a href="http://www.sciencemag.org/cities/#section_podcast">podcast segment</a> and <a href="http://www.sciencemag.org/cities/#section_video">online video presentation</a> that accompanies the issue.<br /><br />For those interested in Cities the issue is well worth a look, see <a href="http://www.sciencemag.org/">http://www.sciencemag.org/</a> for more info.</p>
