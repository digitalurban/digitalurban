<p><a href="http://photos1.blogger.com/blogger2/604/1219/1600/sm_AnimatingCOTrails.jpg"><img style="DISPLAY: block; MARGIN: 0px auto 10px; CURSOR: hand; TEXT-ALIGN: center" alt="" src="/assets/uploads/external/photos1.blogger.com/blogger2/604/1219/400/sm_AnimatingCOTrails.jpg" border="0" /></a><br />Richard Milton, a researcher at CASA working on our <a href="http://www.ncess.ac.uk/nodes/geovue/">GeoVUE project </a>(funded by <a href="http://www.ncess.ac.uk/">NCESS</a>), has produced an interesting use of Google Maps to visualise urban pollution trails.<br /><br />As a result of the write up of his work, in New Scientist, the BBC got in touch to film a sequence based on measuring carbon monoxide levels from a cyclists perspective.<br /><br />The Google Map features a colour coded time line of pollution, animating along the path linked to a java applet. The peak of carbon monoxide emissions is as the presenter got stuck behind a bus..<br /><br />You can <a href="http://www.casa.ucl.ac.uk/bbc/dontdieyoung/log_25-09-2006_154206.html">view the Google Maps example from here</a><br /><br />Richard has written up his account of the experiment on the <a href="http://blog.casa.ucl.ac.uk">CASA Blog</a>.</p>
